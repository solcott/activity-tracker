# Activity files

`core/activityfile` (`:core-activityfile`) reads FIT, GPX, TCX and Shearwater dive (XML) files and UDDF and Subsurface (`.ssrf`/XML) logbooks, optionally gzipped, into `ImportedActivity`s (`ActivityFileReader.read` returns a list: one per file, or one per dive in a logbook), which `ActivityDraft.fromImport` publishes like a recording (its GPX track a `feed.activityTrack` in the athlete's `self` space, `ActivityTrackStore`). Every sample's channels (indoor ones too, plus gear changes) become `ImportedActivity.streams` through `StreamBuilder`, in the lexicon's integer units: besides the motion channels, a dive's ppO₂, CNS, N₂ load, NDL, deco stop depth and time (null outside deco), time to surface and per-tank pressure (`tankPressures`, each tank's readings placed on the nearest sample within 30 s, in `dive.tanks` order), a run's respiration rate and running dynamics, and left/right power balance. The channel list is `ActivityStreamData.INT_CHANNELS` (`core/lexicon`), which `halved()`, `ActivityMerge` and the AppView's `bounded` iterate, so a new int channel joins that table. Gas switches (`gasSwitches`, the gas named as oxygen and helium percents; the first gas is a switch at 0, repeats dropped) and laps (FIT's and TCX's alike, with calories, intensity and trigger as `LapIntensity`/`LapTrigger` tokens; a single lap spanning the activity isn't kept) sit beside them like `gears`, as do a pool swim's `lengths` (an idle length a rest), a workout's `sets` (FIT `set` messages: category and exercise as the FIT profile's tokens in snake case, `bench_press`/`barbell_bench_press`, each decoder resolving the exercise through its category's name type; weight in grams) and a multisport file's `legs` (a session each, FIT's transition sport a leg with `transition`, kept only for more than one session; its laps stay `laps`). `WorkoutStructure` turns them into lexicon shapes and spells their tokens. A ski or snowboard day's FIT `ski_run_split`/`ski_lift_split` splits replace its laps (a lift a `rest` lap), and the page counts its runs. A pool swim's `pool_length` publishes on the record as `poolLength` (cm). TCX reads every `Activity` of its first `MultiSportSession` (a session per activity and per `NextSport` `Transition`, each lasting until the next starts) and sets each lap's elapsed time from where the next one starts; a phone recording gets distance, altitude and speed streams from `StreamBuilder.fromLocations`. They publish as a `feed.activityStreams` record under the activity's rkey (see `ActivityStreamsStore`). `ActivityFileReader` is the entry point: it picks the format from the content, not the file name. GPX, TCX, Shearwater, UDDF and Subsurface XML parse in common code with xmlutil's pull parser (`walkXml` matches local names, so namespace prefixes don't matter); the dive logs share `DiveLogs` (summary, sub-sport, device name, gases) and skip an undated dive rather than fail the logbook. Dive-log times have no zone (`parseLocalDateTime`): they're read in the device's zone (`TimeZones.device()`), `ImportedActivity.localStart` keeps the clock, and the import screen warns and lets the athlete pick the dive's zone (`inZone`; web bundles `@js-joda/timezone` for the zone rules). A dive's depths, gases, CNS, tanks and site come through as `ImportedDive`, from Shearwater files and FIT dive logs alike. Every decoder and parser keeps when each gas was switched to (`DecodedActivity.gasSwitches`): FIT `dive_gas_switched` events (their gas index resolved by `FitDives.gasSwitches`, which drops switches to unknown slots), Subsurface `gaschange` events, UDDF `switchmix` and Shearwater's per-sample gas fractions. FIT `tank_update` and UDDF `tankpressure` readings come through as `DecodedActivity.tankUpdates`, told apart by sensor (FIT tanks are ordered by `tank_summary` sensor). Shearwater and Subsurface fill the same deco sample fields and `tankUpdates`, so their dives chart like FIT and UDDF ones: Shearwater's `averagePPO2`, `currentNdl` and `ttsMins` (minutes, NDL's 99 cap kept), `firstStopDepth` (file units, 0 is out of deco, no stop time) and `tank0pressurePSI`…`tank3pressurePSI` (PSI in either unit system; text such as `AI is off` is no reading), a `DiveTank` per reporting slot (sensor `"0"`…`"3"`, start and end from its readings); Subsurface's sample `ndl`, `tts`, `stopdepth`, `stoptime`, `cns` and `po2`, written only on change and so carried forward, the stop cleared at `in_deco='0'`, and `pressure`/`pressureN` (cylinder `sensorN`, which also holds until changed) by cylinder index, a cylinder with readings but no start or end a tank from its readings. Dive alerts (`streamData.diveAlerts`, `{ time, kind }`, in time order beside `gasSwitches`) come from FIT `dive_alert` events (each decoder passes the raw `data` number, `FitDiveAlert`, and `FitDives.diveAlerts` resolves it, dropping the two dismissals and alerts without a time) and UDDF waypoint `alarm`s. `DiveAlerts` (common) is the one table of kinds: each `DiveAlertKind` token (FIT's `dive_alert` names in lower camel case, then UDDF's own alarms) with its FIT value, its UDDF alarm (`ascent` → `ascentCritical`, `deco` → `decoCeilingBroken`, `surface` → `nearSurface`) and whether it's a warning; a FIT value it lacks is kept as the SDK's name in lower camel case or its number, a UDDF alarm it lacks as its text. A jvmTest checks every Java SDK `DiveAlert` is in it. Only warnings are marked on the depth chart (`ChartsBuilder` → `ActivityCharts.alerts`, drawn in the error colour and labelled along the bottom, `dive_alert_<token>` strings). The dive computer's settings land on `ActivityDive` beside the gradient factors and water type: `decoModel` (`DecoModel`, from FIT `dive_settings`' `model`, which names only ZHL-16C, and Shearwater's `decoModel`, libdivecomputer's 0 GF/ZHL-16C, 1 VPM-B, 2 VPM-B/GFS, 3 DCIEM), `ppO2Warn`/`ppO2Critical`/`ppO2Deco`, `lowSetpoint`/`highSetpoint` (mbar) and `safetyStopTime` (only when the safety stop was on), shown in `DiveStats`' settings. UDDF also gives waypoint ppO₂, CNS, no-deco time, deco stops and heart rate, the dive's air temperature, visibility, altitude, no-fly and desaturation times and highest ppO₂, each tank's volume and gas, and the diver's notes, which seed the import screen's description for a single file (a logbook batch drops them, since the athlete never sees them). A FIT activity's calories, training effects, power, cadence, descent, shift counts and mountain bike dynamics publish on the record; its training load (FTP, IF, TSS, training effects) goes on the zones record (`ActivityZonesStore.put`), never the public activity. A dive with no GPS track keeps its entry and exit (or the logbook site's position) as a GPX track in `self`, with no polyline. A logbook's dive site name comes through as `ImportedDive.siteName` and lands in `ActivityDive.site` (the name only: its coordinates, `entry`/`exit`, stay out of public records), which the athlete can change on the import screen or later with Edit. `DiveSummary.of` turns the rest into `ActivityRecord.dive` in lexicon units (cm, tenths of °C, seconds, mbar, mL), and the dive's page and feed card show those figures (`ui/DiveStats.kt`) instead of distance and pace. A dive logged before that falls back to its depth chart for depths. Each FIT decoder passes its SDK's dive messages through as `FitDiveMessages`, and `FitDives` (common) picks the session's summary and the gases breathed. FIT uses Garmin's own SDK on each platform behind the `FitDecoder` interface: the Java SDK on Android, desktop and the AppView (`commonJvmMain`), the npm JS SDK on web (`@JsModule` externals), and the Swift SDK on iOS. Kotlin/Native can't call Swift, so `iosApp/iosApp/SwiftFitDecoder.swift` implements the interface (exported to Swift by Swift export, with the DTOs as plain Swift classes taking native optionals) and `ContentView` and `iOSApp` pass it to `:iosShell`'s `IosApp`, which hands it to `IosAppGraph.Factory`. It throws the `ActivityFileException` that `FitSupport.unreadable` makes, which reaches Kotlin as that exception, and calls `FitDives`, `FitHeart` and `FitUnits` through `:iosShell`'s `FitSupport`, since Swift sees a `:core-activityfile` type only where an `:iosShell` signature names it. `gunzip` and `gzip` are expect/actual: the browser's `DecompressionStream`/`CompressionStream` on web (native and off the main thread), and kmp-zlib everywhere else (`nonWebMain`, gunzip through `InflaterInput`). `ActivityFileReader.readFile` also reports the file's `ActivityFileFormat` (each carries its `feed.activitySource#format` token) and whether it came gzipped (`ImportedFile`); `read` is `readFile(…).activities`. Every format decodes to a `DecodedActivity`, and `DecodedActivityMapper` (common) interprets it.

**Batches and archives** (feed/batch-import). The import screen picks several files at once (FileKit `FileKitMode.Multiple`), each an `ImportSource` read a range at a time, never whole (`PickedFiles`: FileKit `source()` kept open while reads go forward off the web, `Blob.slice` on it). `BatchReader` (common) opens each as plain, gzip or zip: `ZipArchive` reads a zip's central directory (ZIP64 too) and opens any member; a zip inside a zip is walked by local headers as a stream (`ZipStream`), which works for deflated members whose sizes follow their data and ends at a stored one. Members inflate through `InflaterInput`, raw deflate in common code with kmp-zlib's `Inflater` (the browser's `DecompressionStream` can't be fed a piece at a time), which takes from `BufferedInput`'s buffer only the bytes its data uses so the next header follows. Archive members are picked by extension (`.fit`, `.gpx`, `.tcx`, `.xml`, `.uddf`, `.ssrf`, `.gz`, `.zip`; a Mac's `__MACOSX` left out) and capped per member (`MAX_UNZIPPED_BYTES`), per top-level archive (10 GB decompressed) and per listing (`MAX_ARCHIVE_MEMBERS`). `scan()` is a `Flow<ScanEvent>`: each file's full activities (`Read`, with its `EntryRef`: the source and the member path through nested zips), files listed as `Skipped` with a `SkipReason`, and `Progress`. The presenter keeps a `summary()` of each activity (no samples, streams or depth profile) plus the detail categories it holds, so a batch of thousands never holds their data; `readEach(refs)` reads chosen files again, as found (still gzipped), one pass per nested archive, for the preview sheet and saving. `ExportMetadata` recognises a Strava export (`activities.csv` beside `activities/`, read by `Csv`, keyed by `Filename`) and a Garmin Connect one (`DI_CONNECT/DI-Connect-Fitness/…summarizedActivities….json`, matched by the activity ID in a file's name, else start and length) and gives each activity an `AccountExportActivity`: title, description, type (`StravaTypes`, `GarminTypes`) and `ExportVisibility`, which saving narrows the batch's visibility to, never widens. Real exports often carry no privacy at all (Strava's `activities.csv` has no such column; its `Activity Private Note` is a personal note, never read), so each of `ImportBatch.exports` (one per export picked, so a Strava and a Garmin export together each get a note) says whether to say so. Inside archives, a FIT file of another type or a GPX file with no times (`NotAnActivityFileException`: Garmin's uploads are mostly wellness monitoring, and its `mapdata-*.gpx` saved routes and waypoints) counts as left out, not damaged, and blank space before an XML declaration (some of Strava's TCX files) is trimmed. Two or more activities show as an `ImportBatch` (rows oldest first, grouped by month, filters, a zone per zone-less file, a read-only preview sheet where only title and type change); one keeps the single-file form. Saving persists the plan (`newBatchSave`, see data-and-auth's batch saves) and `BatchSaveRunner` works through it: combine groups first (combining drains the outbox), then the rest read again in one pass over their files (`PlanDrafting`, a nested archive inflated once) and queued one at a time through `ActivityRepository.publishBatch(Flow, drain = false)`, one zones and training read for the whole batch, no draft held once queued; each picked file or archive member is its own `SourceFile`, shared by the activities read from it, the archive itself never stored.

**Originals.** Every imported file is kept as is (`feed.activitySource` in `self`, `ActivitySourceStore` in `core/data`). `SourceFile.prepare(bytes, fileName, file)` gzips the picked bytes unless they came gzipped (`compressed` says which), returns null when the stored form passes 10 MB (the import screen then says the original won't be kept), and gives a random `key` that every draft read from the file shares, so a logbook is stored and uploaded once (each dive's record names its `entry`, in file order). `SourceFile.original(stored, compressed)` gives back the picked bytes for download.

**Devices.** `FitDevices.interpret` (common) gives `ImportedActivity.devices` (`ImportedDevices`, null when the file names none): the recording device first, from the file's header (`DecodedSource`: FIT `file_id`, TCX `Creator` with `UnitId` and `Version`, a dive log's computer with Shearwater's `computerSerial`/`computerFirmware`, UDDF's `serialnumber` or Subsurface's `deviceid`), filled in from FIT's `device_info` index 0 (the JS SDK names it `"creator"`; its decoder maps that to 0), then each paired sensor with its serial number, ANT device number and transmission type, connection, firmware, battery (status, percent, millivolts) and lifetime operating time (`cum_operating_time`). A device's `device_info` messages are merged field by field, the last value winning, because a sensor can send its serial only after the first (a real Edge 840 ride's power meter did); local devices other than the recorder are left out, and a product number with no name is kept as the model. The session's `sport_profile_name` is the `activityProfile`. Unlike `sensors`, which publish make and model on the record, devices go only to `feed.activityDevices` in `self` (`ActivityDevicesStore`). The Edge 840 writes serials but no ANT IDs, and no `bike_profile`.

**Export.** The module also writes GPX, TCX, FIT and, for dives only, UDDF for the owner's Export action (`ActivityExporter` in `core/data`; `formats(…, isDive)` offers UDDF wherever it offers TCX on a dive). `ExportPoints.join` joins the stored track and streams on their second into an `ExportActivity` in SI units (the reverse of `StreamBuilder`), and `GpxWriter`/`TcxWriter` serialize it with `buildString` (GPX: Garmin `TrackPointExtension` v2 plus a bare `<power>`, one segment; TCX: a `Lap` per stored lap with its points, calories, `Resting` for a rest and its trigger, or one spanning lap when none are stored, `TPX` speed, run cadence and watts, and `LX` lap averages). Numbers go through `toXmlDecimal` and text through `escapeXml` (`core/location` `XmlText.kt`, shared with the track blob's `Gpx`), never `Double.toString`. Tests round-trip each writer through `ActivityFileReader`. `FitWriter` writes FIT in common code (`FitOutput`: header, definitions, little-endian fields, CRC), unlike decoding, which uses each platform's SDK: the file holds only messages we choose, with field numbers and scales from Garmin's profile (`FitProfile`), and jvmTests check it by decoding with the Java SDK. It writes one record per whole second (the first point in it, with every stored channel, deco state and running dynamics included), gear changes as events, and a dive's settings (with water type and density, the deco model only for `zhl16c`, ppO₂ limits, setpoints and a stored safety stop as enabled with its time), a `dive_alert` event per alert `DiveAlerts` gives a FIT value, gases (the record's, then any a switch names that it doesn't), a `dive_gas_switched` event per switch naming its gas slot, a `tank_update` per tank reading (tank n is sensor n + 1, as on its `tank_summary`) and `dive_summary`. Stored laps become lap messages (with their calories, intensity, trigger, leg's sport and, on a pool swim, `first_length_index`/`num_lengths`), or one lap spanning the activity when none are stored; a pool swim's lengths become `length` messages and its pool length (metric, or statute for a whole number of yards) and length counts go on the session; a workout's sets become `set` messages (tokens back to FIT values through `FitExercises`, a table generated from the JS SDK's profile, to regenerate on an SDK bump); stored legs become a session each (transitions with FIT's transition sport, `num_sessions` on the activity), else one session carries the record's totals and, as only the owner exports, the zones record's training load (`ActivityExporter` reads it from `getActivityStreams`). A re-imported FIT gives back every gas, switch, FIT-named alert, setting, lap, length, set, leg and channel. `UddfWriter` (common, `buildString`) writes a UDDF 3.2 logbook of one dive: the record's `device` as the diver's dive computer, the site (`dive.site` and the first positioned point's coordinates, which only the owner's export carries), a `mix` per gas (the record's, then any a switch or tank names that it lacks; ids from the percents, `mix-21-0`), then one repetition group and dive with `informationbeforedive` (datetime in the zone `ActivityExporter` passes, `TimeZone.currentSystemDefault()`, as a zone-less local time), a waypoint per second with a depth (the first point in it, so an entry and exit track adds none) carrying every stored dive channel, `switchmix` at the first waypoint at or after each switch and an `alarm` (first in the waypoint) at the first waypoint at or after each alert with a UDDF alarm, its `tankdata` (`tankN` in `dive.tanks` order, which waypoint `tankpressure` refs name, then a gas-only `tankdata` per mix no stored tank holds: Subsurface makes cylinders only from tanks in a namespaced file; `UddfParser` skips such tanks). Subsurface's `uddf.xslt` can't resolve a namespaced file's `switchmix` gas or `divemode`, so it shows the gases but not which one each switch went to, nor CCR mode; the file keeps the UDDF namespace anyway and `informationafterdive`, in SI units. A dive stored without switch times switches to its first gas at the start and only defines the rest. Children follow the 3.2 schema's sequences, which a jvmTest (`UddfSchemaOrderTest`) checks per parent; `heartrate`, which the 3.2.1 schema lacks, goes after `divetime`. `UddfWriterTest` round-trips through `ActivityFileReader`. `FitSports` maps activity types to FIT sport and sub-sport, the reverse of `DecodedActivityMapper.activityType`, and a test checks each entry imports back as its type. `Gpx.write` in `core/location` stays the track blob's writer and doesn't change.

**Sample files.** For checking imports, the activity page and exports by hand, `./gradlew :core-activityfile:jvmTest --tests "*.WriteWorkoutSamples" -PworkoutSamples` writes its samples to `build/workout-fixtures` with `FitWriter`/`TcxWriter`: a pool swim (lengths and a rest), a strength workout (sets and rests), a triathlon (legs and transitions), a ski day (lifts as rest laps), a mountain bike ride (laps with power), a TCX interval run (warm-up, rest laps, cool-down), a TCX duathlon (`MultiSportSession`) and a TCX run sampled every 30 s; three sensor FIT files from `SensorFitFixtures` (Garmin's encoder): a swim whose heart rate is only in `hr` messages, a run with HRV, Stryd developer fields and a UTC+2 clock, and an e-bike ride with cycling dynamics, CORE and Moxy channels; and three dives: `FitFixtures.dive` (alerts, a dismissal and computer settings), a Shearwater dive into deco on one transmitter and a Subsurface deco dive on two cylinders. Without the property the test skips, so `check` writes nothing. Each sample is built from steady stretches and every lap, leg and summary average is taken from the points written; the test reads each file back through `ActivityFileReader` and fails if an average falls outside its samples' range or a lap with distance lacks a speed. Unit tests keep their own small inline fixtures. The lap table also shows a speed for a lap that has none (TCX's `LX/AvgSpeed` is optional): `ChartsBuilder.lapRow` divides its distance by its moving time, else its elapsed time.

**Duplicates.** The import screen loads the athlete's activities of every sport from 14 hours either side of each imported one (`rememberOwnActivities`, `FeedRepository.ownActivitiesAround`: the batch's padded spans merged into windows of at most 31 days, a window with more pages than `ownActivitiesInRange` reads halved and read again, so a batch spanning years is searched whole; each window is the AppView's `getAuthorActivitiesByStart` plus what the device knows of, read even when the AppView can't be (outbox writes, the cached feed, and the upload log's records of the last 7 days); a search that can't reach the AppView goes on locally, says only this device's activities were checked, and offers Retry), and `ActivityMatcher.matchSpans` (`core/data`, over `MatchSpan`s, comparing only activities within 14 hours of each other) flags each imported activity that overlaps a logged one by half the shorter, repeats an earlier one in the batch (`DuplicateOf.InBatch`, whichever file it came from), or, only for a file whose times have no zone (`localStart`, a dive log's), is the same length a whole number of hours off (a zone mix-up). A matched member of a combined activity stands for that combined activity. Each match also says whether it's an exact copy (`ActivityMatch.exactCopy`: starts and lengths within a second, distances within 0.5%, the same device or none named, as two exports of one upload are; never a zone-shifted match) and whether the two share a sport (`sameSport`, sub-sports aside); among several candidates an exact copy wins over the first overlap. Each duplicate offers `DuplicateAction.Ignore`, `ImportAnyway` and `Combine` (`BatchSelection`): an exact copy defaults to Ignore, in a batch (`BatchSelection.batch`) one of another sport to Import anyway so defaults never merge a day's different activities, and the rest to Combine (`Ignore` while the logged activity is still in the outbox, since combining reads it as stored). A single file keeps Combine for another sport. On the Duplicates filter, `ShownDuplicatesAction` sets every shown duplicate at once, and an opened duplicate's preview compares it with what it repeats (`ImportItemPreview.matched`). Combine saves the import as its own activity and combines it with the logged one through `ActivityManager.combine(into, drafts)`, which builds from the drafts' own data so nothing waits for the AppView; within a batch, every copy of one activity joins the first copy's combined activity (`combine(drafts)`, `batchPlan`), a chain passing through a copy the athlete left out to what it repeats. Merging into a logged activity and grouping (`recordingOf`) are gone: clients never write `recordingOf`, `LegacyGroupMigration` turns existing groups into combined activities once after sign-in, and the AppView and `RecordingsUi` still honour groups not yet migrated. "Add a recording…" on the owner's activity page opens the import screen with `combineInto`: no duplicate search or visibility pickers, a logbook offers only its entry nearest the activity, and a file starting more than 12 hours from it is refused. On its page, the owner can delete an activity (`ActivityManager.delete`, confirmed in an `AppAlertDialog`): a legacy primary's recordings go with it or, if the athlete chooses, are split off first (`recordingOf` cleared) so none points at a deleted activity. A legacy group of exactly two can be ungrouped from either page (`ActivityManager.ungroup`).
