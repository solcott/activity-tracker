# AppView

- **The AppView never writes to repos or spaces. It holds non-public activities, by tier, likes and comments on them, athletes' blocks and mutes, and, for athletes who turned segment matching on, their zone-trimmed segment tracks, the segment efforts and candidate riders matched from them, two leaderboard choices (hiding from every board, excluding an activity) and, for athletes who chose bracket boards, their leaderboard brackets (age group, weight class, gender), athletes' non-public gear items, their mounts and the gear their activities list, and nothing else private.** It reads Jetstream for public records, syncs athletes' tier spaces for non-public activities and their `interactions` spaces for likes and comments on them, hides of comments on their own, and their blocks and mutes (below), and serves `app.activitytracker.*` queries. From tier spaces it stores only `feed.activity`, `feed.activityDetails`, `feed.activityStreams`, `feed.activityZones` and `gear.item`, from the `self` space also `feed.segmentTrack` and `actor.segmentPrefs` (each with its leaderboard choice) and `actor.leaderboardBrackets`, and from `interactions` spaces only `feed.like`, `feed.comment`, `feed.commentHide`, `graph.block` and `graph.mute` (`Indexer.spaceCollections`): never `actor.privateDetails`, `graph.followDecline`, `actor.activityDefaults`, `gear.autoAssign` (the athlete's gear defaults per activity type, in `self`), `actor.privacyZones`, `actor.routeTrim`, `feed.segmentStar` (the athlete's private stars on segments, in `self`), `gear.collection` (the athlete's named groups of their gear items, in `self`), `feed.activityTrack` (an activity's full track), `feed.activitySource` (an imported activity's original file) or `feed.activityDevices` (its devices and their serial numbers), all three kept in `self` for the athlete alone. Polylines reach it already trimmed by the athlete's privacy zones, at every visibility. **Segment tracks** (`feed.segmentTrack`, `segment_tracks`) are clients' copies of a track with the privacy zones and hidden ends cut out, in pieces, timed in seconds from the track's start: the indexer keeps one only for the author's own activity under the same rkey, with a data blob, whatever the setting (`actor.segmentPrefs`, `segment_prefs`), since ops arrive in any order; anything that reads their data must go through `usableSegmentTracks()`, which joins the setting and admits only matching on. A setting of off, or deleting it, drops the athlete's segment tracks in the same transaction, as do `dropSpace` of `self` and `deleteActor`. No route serves either: their only uses are matching segment efforts and counting riders on staged candidates (below). **Leaderboard brackets** (`actor.leaderboardBrackets`, `leaderboard_brackets`, segments/leaderboard-brackets) are computed on the athlete's device and exist only while they've chosen bracket boards: the AppView never reads birth date, weight or gender from any record, stores a bracket value it doesn't know as NULL, and drops the row when the record is deleted, with `dropSpace` of `self` and in `deleteActor` (matching off leaves it, since it drops every effort anyway). Its only use is filtering leaderboards. Profile details an athlete marks Followers, Mutuals or Only me stay in their tier spaces, which clients read directly, so views show only the profile record's details (`publicDetails`, `api/ActorDetails.kt`). The indexer validates those with `ProfileDetailRules` and drops what breaks a rule. Treat the database and its backups as holding private data.
- **Segments and efforts** (`segments/segment-records`, `segments/segment-efforts`). A `feed.segment` is public and indexed from the repo only (`index/SegmentRecordIndex.kt`; `spaceCollections` never lists it): the AppView decodes its precision-6 line, computes its length (its sport group's minimum, `SegmentSportGroup.minMeters`, to 50 km, else dropped) and bounding box, and keeps the 0.01° cells it passes (`segment_cells`, `SegmentCells` in `core/location`), since there's no PostGIS on H2. A creator's eleventh new segment in 24 hours (by `first_indexed_at`) is dropped too, except the segment importer's (`SEGMENT_IMPORTER_DID`, passed to `Indexer` and `SegmentViews`; `checkSegment` always gives it the full allowance). Its segments are keyed by candidate id (`feed.segment`'s key is `any`; nothing here reads segment rkeys as TIDs). **Statuses are derived, never read from the record** (`segments.status`, `duplicate_of`, `first_indexed_at`, which an edit never changes): a new or re-lined segment is `duplicate` of the earliest-first-indexed `proposed`/`active` segment of its sport whose ends lie in its end cells and that `SegmentGeometry.duplicates` matches (decided inline in the indexer, so a segment is never served without one), else `proposed`; a deleted, archived or re-lined segment's duplicates are checked again in order (`recheckDuplicatesOf`). `SegmentMatcher` promotes a proposed segment to `active` once `SEGMENT_ACTIVE_ATHLETES` (default 3) distinct athletes have efforts on it, counting every tier, and its hourly status pass promotes and archives (proposed for 180 days); active is never undone. Only `proposed`/`active` segments are matched; a duplicate loses its efforts, an archived one keeps them. `checkSegment` runs the same validation and duplicate test for a signed-in client before it publishes, and writes nothing. `getSegment` and `searchSegments` (a box of at most 1°, cells then the stored box, then each line checked against the box) hide a segment through `served`; search lists `active` segments, and `proposed` ones only to their creator and athletes with an effort on them (`listedTo`). **Efforts are derived private data.** `SegmentMatcher` (`segments/`, started only with a signing key) works a durable queue, `segment_match_jobs` keyed by `(kind, subject_uri)`, which the indexer fills in the transaction of each change (`SegmentJobs`): a segment-track put or matching turned on queues `track`, a new or changed segment `segment` (a changed line or sport deletes its efforts first), and an activity, details or streams change for an activity with efforts or a usable track `tiers`, or `track` when its sport, sub-sport or member claims changed (`index/MatchInputs.kt`). Deletes never queue: a deleted track, activity, segment or account, matching off and a dropped `self` delete the efforts at once (`dropSegmentTracks`). A `track` job fetches the blob through `SpaceBlobs` only for a track `usableSegmentTracks()` admits, skips a valid member of a combined activity (`combinedIntoOf`) and an activity with no `SegmentSport` group, records the track's cells (`segment_track_cells`, so a later segment finds past tracks without fetching every blob), runs `SegmentGeometry.passes` against the segments of its group sharing a cell, and, after checking again that the track is still usable and unchanged, replaces the activity's efforts in one transaction. Failed fetches back off up to an hour; the first start queues every usable track with no cells. A blob that isn't a valid track (`piecesOf`: undecodable, arrays that don't line up, times negative or going backwards; repeated whole seconds are fine) isn't retried: the job drops the activity's efforts, the track's cells and its candidate riders. Passes starting in the same rounded second on one segment are one effort, keyed by that offset. An effort's `visibility` is the narrowest of the activity's and its Map and Pace/Speed categories' tiers (`DetailCategory.currentTiersOfChannels`, from indexed rows only), kept current by `tiers` jobs, and every effort query applies `visibleTo` on the effort and on its activity's row (so a narrowed or taken-down activity hides its efforts before its job runs) and `served` on the segment; `best` is judged among the efforts the same viewer may see. `getActivitySegments` is `NotFound` for an activity the viewer can't see. Efforts are never stored with a wall-clock time: only the offset from the activity's start, the elapsed time, and the local date (`started_on`). **Leaderboards** (`segments/segment-leaderboards`): `getSegmentLeaderboard` ranks each athlete's fastest effort per board (a segment and an activity type: the effort's activity's `sport`, read live through the join, or `eBike` from `segment_efforts.e_bike`; `SegmentBoard` in `core/lexicon`) among those `effortsVisibleTo` admits for the viewer and `rankable()` allows: the segment is `active`, the track isn't `leaderboard_excluded` (`feed.segmentTrack#excludeFromLeaderboards`), the author doesn't `hide_from_leaderboards` (`actor.segmentPrefs`), and `elapsed_s * SegmentSportGroup.maxSpeedMmPerS >= distance_m * 1000`. The `ageGroup`, `weightClass` and `gender` filters (`bracketsOf`) add an `exists` on `leaderboard_brackets` matching each one given, so athletes without brackets are on no filtered board and ranks count only the bracket; an unknown value is `InvalidRequest`. Each response also lists the board types (`boardsOf`: one grouped count over the segment's visible, rankable efforts, ignoring period, scope and brackets) and serves the requested one, listed even with no efforts, or else the busiest; an unknown type is `InvalidRequest`. All of it is read when the board is, so no flag or cap change needs a rematch, and a segment-track put that changes only the flag (same `data_cid` and zones revision) queues no `track` job. Boards page by a `GROUP BY author_did … HAVING` keyset on `(MIN(elapsed_s), author_did)`, and ranks (competition: ties share, the next skips) come from counting the groups ahead, so there's no window function; periods compare `started_on` with the AppView's UTC date, and the `following` scope is the viewer plus `effectiveFollow()`s. **Mayors** (`segments/segment-mayors`): `getSegmentMayors` counts the same efforts per athlete, over the same `boardOf` predicate and type list (with an exclusive `until` for the "last" windows, `mayorWindow`), and orders by `COUNT` descending then `MAX(started_on)` ascending (the first to reach a count leads), paged and ranked with the same `HAVING` keyset and `ranksOf`; it reads nothing the time boards don't and stores nothing. `getActivitySegments` adds a best effort's `rank` when it's in the top 10 of the viewer's all-time board of the effort's own type, and `notRanked` (hidden, excluded, implausible) only for the athlete's own efforts on active segments. Neither choice is ever served otherwise. **Candidates** (`segments/segment-candidates`, `api/SegmentCandidateViews.kt`): the importer (`SEGMENT_IMPORTER_DID`; with none set every caller gets `403 NotImporter`) stages public candidate lines by region and revision (`segment_candidates`, `segment_candidate_cells`), validated with `validLine`/`validName` and rejected per candidate, never per batch; an unchanged line and sport keeps riders and `ridden`, a changed one clears them, replaces its cells and queues a `candidate` job (which, like `segment`, queues `track` jobs for usable tracks sharing its cells). `put` validates a batch before its transaction and writes it set-based (one lookup, batched upserts, deletes and cell inserts, one batched job upsert), so its round trips don't grow with the batch; keep it that way, since a batch that outlasts the importer's 60 s request timeout is rolled back. `finishSegmentCandidates` deletes the region's other revisions with cells and riders. No feed query, effort, status or duplicate check reads these tables. After `replaceEfforts`, a `track` job replaces the track's rows in `segment_candidate_riders` with the candidates of its group in its cells that `SegmentGeometry.passes` from start to end (every tier counts), then sets `ridden` once `SEGMENT_CANDIDATE_RIDERS` (default 3) distinct authors have a row; ridden is never undone. Riders are dropped everywhere efforts are: `trackToMatch`'s `none()`, a gone activity (`activityChanged`, `retier`), `dropTracks` and `dropSegmentTracks`. `getRiddenSegmentCandidates` pages ridden candidates by id, leaving out each one the importer already published (a `segments` row at `at://<importer>/feed.segment/<candidate id>`, any status; segments/segment-import), and holds only what was staged: no counts, athletes or dates. `checkSegmentCandidates` runs `checkSegment`'s duplicate test (`duplicatesOf`, against the segments `served` to the importer) on up to 500 of a region's staged candidates named by id, in one transaction, leaves out ids not staged there, and writes nothing; `publish` calls it once per page instead of once per candidate.
- **Every activity read is filtered by tier.** `activities.visibility` is the tier a row is served at (`public` for the repo, else its space's key), and `visibleTo(viewer)` (`api/TierAccess.kt`) ANDs into every activity query: timeline, author activities (both orders), `getActivity` (a hidden activity is `NotFound`), `getLikes`, `getComments`, recordings and `activitiesCount`. It admits readers as `SpaceAccess` does, from effective follows; an unknown tier is the athlete's only, and a null viewer sees public rows. Likes are checked at serve time for their giver too: `hydrate`'s counts and `getLikes` join likes to the activity (`admittedLikes`) and apply `visibleTo(Likes.authorDid)`, the per-row form, so a like counts only while its giver may see the activity at its current tier, wherever the like is stored (repo or the giver's `interactions` space, `likes.origin`). Comments get the same giver check plus their audience: `hydrate`'s counts and `getComments`' list join comments to the activity (`admittedComments`) and apply `commentsVisibleTo`, which is `visibleTo(Comments.authorDid)` and `visibleTo(viewer)` against `comments.audience` (the visibility the comment was written for, normalised at index time: absent is `public`, unknown is `self`), and for a reply also against its thread's root's audience while the root is indexed on the same activity (`rootAdmits`, through the `Roots` alias `admittedComments` left-joins). Clients cap a reply by its parent, so only the root is checked here: a dishonest client's wider reply still never reaches past it. Tiers nest, so a comment shows at the narrower of the activity's current tier and its audience: it follows a narrowing, but a widening never shows it wider than written. `comments.origin` scopes deletes and `dropSpace` like `likes.origin`. An athlete hides a comment on their own activity with `feed.commentHide` (`comment_hides`, keyed by `(uri, origin)`, so the repo's and the space's copies are separate rows). The indexer keeps a hide only at rkey = `commentHideRkey(subject)` (`core/atproto`), checked before its transaction since the hash suspends; whether its author is the activity's athlete is checked at serve time (`hiddenByAthlete`), so a hide may arrive before its comment and a stranger's is inert. A hidden comment is never counted (`hydrate` ANDs `not(hiddenByAthlete())`) and is listed, `hidden = true`, only for the athlete and its commenter (`commentsListedFor`). Every comment query, count or list, must apply both, or the count stops matching the listed comments that aren't hidden. `getActivity` lists no comments (it still writes `comments: []` for older clients). **Replies are comment rows in a thread.** Threads are one level deep: `comments.thread_uri` is the record's `reply.root` (its own `uri` for a top-level comment), `parent_uri` its `reply.parent`, and `thread_cid`/`parent_cid` complete the strong refs views serve. The indexer skips a reply whose root or parent isn't a `feed.comment` URI with an rkey (dropping an earlier version of it from the same origin, so an edit can't keep it listed), but doesn't check that the root is indexed or on the same activity: threads are grouped within `subject_uri`, so a stray root just leaves its replies under a placeholder. `getComments` lists them oldest first in three modes, all through the same `commentsListedFor` filter, so the count (which includes replies) matches. A page (`threadPage`) groups the listed rows by `thread_uri` with a `GROUP BY … HAVING` keyset on `(MIN(threadPlace), thread_uri)`. A root is placed by its `created_at`, and a reply by the later of its `created_at` and its first `indexed_at` (comment upserts keep that one on replay), so a backdated reply can't move its thread earlier and a new reply never moves a thread across a page already read. A thread whose root isn't listed (a placeholder, `root` absent, revealing nothing) is placed by its earliest listed reply. The page then reads each thread's first three listed replies with a limited query per thread, plus one grouped count for `replyCount`, and sets a `repliesCursor` when there are more. `comments` holds the page's top-level comments for older clients. With `root`, a page of that thread's replies (a keyset on `(created_at, uri)` with `keysetAfter`, whose cursor comes from the last row read; `commentViews` builds a view for every row, showing an author with no `actors` row as `handle.invalid`). With `uris`, just those comments and replies (at most 25, no cursor), which clients use to tell whether the AppView has caught up with their own changes on pages they haven't loaded; `uris` with `root` is `InvalidRequest`. The grouping reads every listed row on the activity (`comments_subject_idx`; `HAVING` can't seek), while the reply fetches use `comments_thread_idx`. A recording counts (hidden from feeds, listed under its primary) only at its primary's visibility.
- **Activities have one URI wherever they're held.** A space-held activity is indexed at `at://{did}/app.activitytracker.feed.activity/{rkey}`, like a repo one, with `origin` = `repo` or the space URI. Mid-move one URI has two origins: the narrower tier's copy is the row, the other waits in `record_shadows`, and a delete removes only its own origin's copy, promoting the shadow (`Indexer.place`/`delete`). Zones work the same way (`activity_zones.record` keeps the raw record for that). Streams and details don't: an activity has one of each per tier (`feed/detail-visibility`), so `activity_streams` and `activity_details` are keyed by `(uri, origin)`, each origin's copy a row of its own, and a delete removes only its own origin's row. `feed.activityDetails` is indexed only from tier spaces; a repo copy is ignored.
- **Gear** (`gear/activity-gear`). `gear.item` is indexed from repos (Jetstream) and every tier space into `gear_items`, placed by origin like activities (`heldCopy`, `record_shadows`), its name and type required and clamped, its photo kept only as `boundedPhotos` allows. An activity's `gear` (on its record, or on a details record when its Gear category is narrower) is indexed into `activity_gear` by origin and source (`record` or `details`), keeping only its author's own `gear.item` URIs (`replaceActivityGear`); the record's references follow the activity's one indexed copy, the details' their own origin, and deletes and `dropSpace` remove them. `hydrate` serves `activityView.gear` (`api/GearViews.kt`, `gearOf`): the list of the narrowest record listing gear in a tier `visibleTo` admits the viewer to, keeping only items that still exist and that `visibleTo` admits on the item's own row, and `withGearOnly` strips every other URI from `record` and `details`, so a viewer never learns of an item they may not see. Gear photos go through `/img/media` like activity photos (`gearPhotoRows`): a public item's redirects, a non-public one's is signed for the viewer and fetched from its space. **Mounts** (`gear/gear-components`): each held item's `mounts` go into `gear_mounts` (`GearMountRows`, epoch ms, `replaceGearMounts` inside the item's `place` write, so a promoted shadow rewrites them), keeping only those `GearMounts.kept` allows against the rows already indexed: a parent of the same author, no overlap, no loop, at most `GearMounts.MAX_DEPTH` levels. Each refused mount is dropped, never the item, and a mount whose parent isn't indexed yet is kept. The item's delete, `dropSpace` (through it) and `deleteActor` remove them.
- **Gear usage** (`gear/gear-usage`). `gear.getGear`, `getActorGear` and `getGearActivities` (`Views`, helpers in `api/GearViews.kt`) total an item's use live from `activities` columns the indexer copies out of each record (`index/ActivitySummary`: elapsed and moving time, distance, elevation gain and loss, dive bottom time, `started_on` local epoch day and `started_year`), never from stored aggregates. One rule picks the activities counted (`counted`): those `visibleTo` admits the viewer to that list the item in a record whose tier `visibleTo` admits them to (`visibleRefs`, distinct per activity and item, so a reference at two origins mid-move counts once), less valid members of a combined activity (`isMember`). `visibleRefs` is a `UNION` of the references listing the item and, for each depth up to `GearMounts.MAX_DEPTH`, those listing an item it was mounted on (`mountedRefs`, unrolled self-joins of `gear_mounts`, no recursive CTE): every mount's window holds the activity's `started_at` and `visibleTo` admits the viewer to every `gear_items` row above the counted item, so a deleted or hidden parent counts nothing, and the union keeps each activity once per item. Totals, the by-sport and by-year breakdowns and the activity list all use it, so they always agree; the athlete is admitted to everything. An item the viewer may not see is `NotFound` (`requireVisibleGear`). Starting usage (`gear_items.starting_distance`, `starting_time`) is added in Kotlin to the totals only, never the breakdowns. `getActorGear` totals a page of items with one grouped query by `gear_uri`, keyset `"<0|1 retired>::<uri>::<name>"`; `getGearActivities` pages newest first on `(started_at, uri)`. `getGear` returns the item's visibility only to its author. It also lists the item's `components` (`gearComponents`: current, then previous, each with its totals) and its own `mounts` (`gearMountViews`), each only for items `visibleTo` admits.
- **Activity details** are served in `activityView.details` (`Views.detailsOf`, one batched query per page): only rows whose tier `visibleTo` admits the viewer, for activities the viewer may see, widest first. Clients lay them over `record`.
- **Activity streams are served only through `ActivityStreamsViews`.** `getActivityStreams` serves the union of the activity's streams rows the viewer is admitted to, merged widest first with a narrower tier's channels winning and laps and legs merged by index (`mergeStreams` in `core/lexicon`), lists each in `parts`, and gives `visibility` as the widest. It checks each row's visibility, which is where the record is stored, as for activities (`public` in the repo, the space's tier otherwise, an unknown tier `self`; a `visibility` field in the record is ignored), and hides streams whose indexed activity the viewer can't see. Zones are a record of their own (`feed.activityZones`, `activity_zones`), which the athlete stores at the narrowest of the heart rate, power and training visibilities; `getActivityStreams` includes them only when `visibleTo` admits the viewer to the zones row's own tier. Zone fields in a streams record are ignored. The streams' data blob comes through `StreamSource` (the repo's PDS, or the space as a member) and is cached by CID in `activity_stream_data`. An activity's parts are fetched at once, and concurrent views share one fetch per source (CID, author, origin). A failed fetch is remembered in `activity_stream_fetch_failures` by source: data that isn't stream data or is too large is never fetched again under its CID, and missing or unreachable data only after a pause doubling from a minute to an hour; a successful fetch forgets it. The cleanup that drops cache rows no streams row names drops those failures too.
- **`checkUserAccess` is the managing-app callout.** An athlete's PDS calls it before issuing a credential to one of their `app.activitytracker.space.athlete` spaces. Its tokens are addressed to `managingAppService` (`<serviceDid>#activitytracker_space`), so the route uses the separate managing-app verifier (`verifier.forAudiences`), and AppView tokens never work there or the reverse. `SpaceAccess` decides by tier (`AthleteSpace` skey): `public` admits anyone, `followers` uses `follows` (an effective follow of the athlete, also the rule for Followers streams), `mutuals` uses `isMutual` (effective follows both ways; both in `api/ActorDetails.kt`, also the rule for Mutuals streams), and everything else, every `write` and an issuer other than the space's authority are denied. The AppView's own DID may read all four tiers, `self` included, and the `interactions` space (which admits no one else), so it can sync them; it's denied `write` and unknown keys like anyone. A denial is `200 {"authorized": false}`, never an error.
- **Blocks hide two athletes from each other; mutes hide one from the muter.** `blocks` holds an athlete's `graph.block` from their interactions space (`origin` its URI) and their `app.bsky.graph.block` from their repo (`origin` `repo`), each kept only at rkey = subject for the app's. A pair is blocked while any row names it either way (`blocked()`, `api/Blocks.kt`), and that sits in the shared predicates: `admits` (so `visibleTo`, Public included, and through the giver check every like and comment between them), `effectiveFollow()` (so tier spaces, counts, lists and the timeline) and `SpaceAccess.canRead` (every key). Viewer-versus-giver checks on third parties' activities are `notBlocked`/`notMuted` in `commentsVisibleTo` and the like queries; search, follow lists and `getProfile` filter by the requested DID, and an athlete who blocks the viewer answers `ProfileNotFound`, never "blocked" (`viewer.blocking` is only the viewer's own). Mutes (`mutes`) apply only to the muter's timeline and comments. Jetstream sends every Bluesky block on the network, so `Indexer` keeps one only when its author already has an `actors` row, before `ensureActor`, and `BskyBlockBackfill` lists an actor's existing ones from their PDS while `actors.bsky_blocks_synced_at` is null. Nothing is refused at index time: hiding happens when reading, so an unblock restores everything.
- **Only effective follows count.** An athlete whose profile has `followPolicy: approve` (stored as `actors.follow_policy`; any other value is null, meaning open) counts a follow only once they publish a `graph.followApproval` for the follower. Every query that reads `follows`, except for the viewer's own follow record (`viewer.following`), ANDs in `effectiveFollow()` (`api/ActorDetails.kt`): timeline authors, counts, follower and follow lists, `follows`/`isMutual`. The indexer keeps an approval only at rkey = its subject's DID, so there's one per pair and deleting it removes the follower. `getFollowRequests` lists the pending ones, declined included: declines live in the athlete's `self` space and never reach the AppView.
- **Hosts from identities go through the guarded client.** Anything the AppView fetches from a host it learned from a DID document or a handle (a PDS endpoint, a `did:web` document, a handle's `.well-known`) uses `GuardedHttp`'s client (`net/GuardedHttp.kt`), which refuses private, loopback and link-local addresses unless `APPVIEW_ALLOW_PRIVATE_ADDRESSES` is set (local dev only), or `identity`, the resolver built on it. The CIO client and `configuredIdentity` are only for endpoints the operator configures (PLC, the handle resolver, Jetstream, labelers, Ozone).
- **One indexer per URI at a time.** `Indexer` holds a striped lock by AT-URI around each record's write or delete transaction, so Jetstream and the space syncer never interleave on one URI mid-move; it assumes a single AppView process. The syncer's writes (`spaceWrite(…, whileSynced = true)`) lock the space's `space_sync` row and index nothing once it's gone, and `deleteActor` deletes those rows first, so a sync still running can't bring a deleted account's records back.
- **Repos are untrusted input.** The `Indexer` decodes with the shared `AtJson` models, drops records that fail to decode (it catches `IllegalArgumentException` and `SerializationException`), and truncates free-text columns with `.take(n)` so they fit the column. New record handlers do the same.
- **Indexing is idempotent.** Every write is an upsert or delete keyed by AT-URI, so rewinding the Jetstream cursor replays safely. Never use a plain `insert` for record rows. Every new record table also needs a delete branch in `Indexer.delete` and a line in `deleteActor`.
- **Recordings (`feed.activity#recordingOf`) are honored only within one author's repo.** The indexer stores `recording_of` only for a link to another of the author's own activities. `shownInFeeds` (`api/Views.kt`) hides a recording from feeds only while the activity it names is indexed and isn't itself a recording, and `hydrate` lists a primary's `recordings`. Queries that look for duplicates (`getAuthorActivitiesByStart`) return recordings too.
- **Combined activities (`feed.activity#combined`) are derived from the combined record alone; members are never rewritten.** The indexer stores each listed member of the author's own (at most 10, not itself) as a claim in `activity_members`, replaced when the combined record is re-indexed and dropped with it. Whether a claim counts is checked at read time (`api/Members.kt`, `validClaim`), since members may be indexed after their combined activity and move tier on their own: the combined activity is indexed with the member's author and visibility, the member claims nothing itself, and no smaller (earlier-TID) combined URI of the same author and visibility claims it. `shownInFeeds` and `activitiesCount` leave out validly claimed members (`isMember`), `hydrate` serves `members` and `combinedInto`, and a combined activity's like and comment counts, `getLikes` and `getComments` take its members' too (`interactionSubjects`), under the same giver, audience and hide rules. `getAuthorActivitiesByStart` still returns members, with `combinedInto`, for duplicate searches.
- **The AppView syncs athletes' tier spaces as a member** (`sync/SpaceSyncer.kt`), with its own identity: `APPVIEW_SIGNING_KEY` (P-256, hex; `scripts/dev.sh` generates it into `docker/.env`, the Gradle `run` task reads it) is published as `#atproto` in `did.json` and signs the delegation tokens `SpaceCredentials` exchanges (`SignedDelegationTokens`, `aud` the authority's `#atproto_pds`, `kid` `#atproto`). Without the key nothing is synced and only public activities exist. Clients announce a space with `notifySpace` (service auth from its authority; only `followers`, `mutuals`, `self` and `interactions`); the syncer joins it, registers for `notifyWrite` at `<serviceDid>#activitytracker_space_syncer` (its own audience, so AppView and managing-app tokens never work there), backfills (head revision from `listRepoOps`, then `listRecords`, since the op log may be compacted) and then pulls ops after the last indexed revision. Every `listRepoOps` goes through `SpaceMember.listRepoOps`, which asks for a MAC with a fresh key share per page and verifies the commit's (naming the space and repo asked for) against the authority's `#atproto_kem`; a pull reads every page first and applies its ops and revision only after the commit verifies, so a failed MAC (after one retry with the DID document resolved again) indexes nothing and backs off. `listRecords` carries no MAC, by the protocol; a backfill's revision comes from the verified head. A space that hasn't synced for an hour (backoff, downtime) is backfilled again instead, dropping indexed records it no longer lists, since the ops it missed may be compacted away. The authority's PDS sends each notification once, so every space is also pulled every 15 minutes and its registration renewed 2 h before expiry. A failing space backs off on its own. When an athlete deletes a space, their PDS calls `notifySpaceDeleted` (same audience and authority check as `notifyWrite`, retried until delivered), and `SpaceSyncer.deleted` drops it and its rows before answering, under the space's lock so a sync in progress can't index anything after the drop; a repeat or an unknown space is a no-op. A sync answered `SpaceDeleted` drops it the same way, as the fallback for a notification that never arrived. A PDS caches DID documents, so a new or rotated key is picked up only on a later retry.
- **Activity photos (`feed.activity#media`).** `boundedPhotos` keeps the `#image` members whose blob is WebP or JPEG, within the lexicon's size, with a real CID (other union members stay in the repo but not the index), and `activity_media` lists them. On a public activity, `GET /img/media/{did}/{cid}` (`api/BlobRedirects.kt`) redirects to the PDS's `sync.getBlob`, like the avatar route. A non-public activity's photo URL is signed for the viewer the view was built for (`PrivateMediaTokens`, since image loaders send no service auth); the route checks the signature, re-checks `visibleTo`, fetches the blob from the space as a member (`SpaceBlobs`, which keeps recent blobs in memory by CID) and answers `Cache-Control: private`, never redirecting.
- **Don't trust client timestamps for ordering.** Feed order uses `sort_at = min(createdAt, indexedAt)`, so a client can't pin a record to the top by dating it in the future.
- **Migrations.** These are Flyway files in `src/main/resources/db/migration`, named `V<n>__<description>.sql`. Never edit a migration that is already committed. Add the next `V<n+1>`, because the local Postgres has already applied the old one and Flyway rejects a changed checksum. Keep SQL portable: tests run it on H2 in `MODE=PostgreSQL`, so use no Postgres-only types or functions. Timestamps are `BIGINT` epoch millis. SQL that only PostgreSQL can run (extensions, GIN/trigram indexes) goes in `db/vendor/postgresql`, which `DatabaseFactory.migrate` adds for that database only. Its versions share one sequence with `db/migration` (`MigrationVersionsTest` checks). A backfill that must read record JSON (which SQL can't parse portably) is a Kotlin `JavaMigration` registered in `DatabaseFactory.migrate` (`javaMigrations`), such as V37 `ActivitySummaryBackfill`; its version shares the sequence too.
- **Routes.** Add queries with `xrpcQuery(X.method, verifier) { params, viewer -> ... }` in `Routes.kt`. Auth is optional at that level. Procedures are the sync callouts (`notifySpace`, `notifyWrite`, `notifySpaceDeleted`, in `spaceSyncRoutes` via `xrpcProcedure`), `checkSegment`, a POST only because a line doesn't fit in a query string, which writes nothing, and the importer's `putSegmentCandidates`/`finishSegmentCandidates` (`segmentCandidateRoutes`), which write only the AppView's own staging tables, and its `checkSegmentCandidates`, which writes nothing: clients write records to their own repos and spaces, never through the AppView. A handler that needs a viewer throws `AuthException`. Map client errors to `XrpcError(status, "ErrorName", message)`.
- **Tests** (`AppViewTest`) feed `JetstreamEvent.Commit`s into a real `Indexer` over H2 and then read back through `testApplication`. Test new indexing and views the same way, end to end.
