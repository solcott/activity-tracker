# AppView

- **The AppView never writes to repos or spaces. It holds non-public activities, by tier, likes and comments on them, athletes' blocks and mutes, and, for athletes who turned segment matching on, their zone-trimmed segment tracks and the segment efforts matched from them, and nothing else private.** It reads Jetstream for public records, syncs athletes' tier spaces for non-public activities and their `interactions` spaces for likes and comments on them, hides of comments on their own, and their blocks and mutes (below), and serves `app.activitytracker.*` queries. From tier spaces it stores only `feed.activity`, `feed.activityDetails`, `feed.activityStreams` and `feed.activityZones`, from the `self` space also `feed.segmentTrack` and `actor.segmentPrefs`, and from `interactions` spaces only `feed.like`, `feed.comment`, `feed.commentHide`, `graph.block` and `graph.mute` (`Indexer.spaceCollections`): never `actor.privateDetails`, `graph.followDecline`, `actor.activityDefaults`, `actor.privacyZones`, `actor.routeTrim`, `feed.activityTrack` (an activity's full track), `feed.activitySource` (an imported activity's original file) or `feed.activityDevices` (its devices and their serial numbers), all three kept in `self` for the athlete alone. Polylines reach it already trimmed by the athlete's privacy zones, at every visibility. **Segment tracks** (`feed.segmentTrack`, `segment_tracks`) are clients' copies of a track with the privacy zones and hidden ends cut out, in pieces, timed in seconds from the track's start: the indexer keeps one only for the author's own activity under the same rkey, with a data blob, whatever the setting (`actor.segmentPrefs`, `segment_prefs`), since ops arrive in any order; anything that reads their data must go through `usableSegmentTracks()`, which joins the setting and admits only matching on. A setting of off, or deleting it, drops the athlete's segment tracks in the same transaction, as do `dropSpace` of `self` and `deleteActor`. No route serves either: their only use is matching segment efforts (below). Profile details an athlete marks Followers, Mutuals or Only me stay in their tier spaces, which clients read directly, so views show only the profile record's details (`publicDetails`, `api/ActorDetails.kt`). The indexer validates those with `ProfileDetailRules` and drops what breaks a rule. Treat the database and its backups as holding private data.
- **Segments and efforts** (`segments/segment-records`, `segments/segment-efforts`). A `feed.segment` is public and indexed from the repo only (`index/SegmentRecordIndex.kt`; `spaceCollections` never lists it): the AppView decodes its precision-6 line, computes its length (200 m–50 km, else dropped) and bounding box, and keeps the 0.01° cells it passes (`segment_cells`, `SegmentCells` in `core/location`), since there's no PostGIS on H2. `getSegment` and `searchSegments` (a box of at most 1°, cells then the stored box, then each line checked against the box) hide a segment through `served`. **Efforts are derived private data.** `SegmentMatcher` (`segments/`, started only with a signing key) works a durable queue, `segment_match_jobs` keyed by `(kind, subject_uri)`, which the indexer fills in the transaction of each change (`SegmentJobs`): a segment-track put or matching turned on queues `track`, a new or changed segment `segment` (a changed line or sport deletes its efforts first), and an activity, details or streams change for an activity with efforts or a usable track `tiers`, or `track` when its sport, sub-sport or member claims changed (`index/MatchInputs.kt`). Deletes never queue: a deleted track, activity, segment or account, matching off and a dropped `self` delete the efforts at once (`dropSegmentTracks`). A `track` job fetches the blob through `SpaceBlobs` only for a track `usableSegmentTracks()` admits, skips a valid member of a combined activity (`combinedIntoOf`) and an activity with no `SegmentSport` group, records the track's cells (`segment_track_cells`, so a later segment finds past tracks without fetching every blob), runs `SegmentGeometry.passes` against the segments of its group sharing a cell, and, after checking again that the track is still usable and unchanged, replaces the activity's efforts in one transaction. Failed fetches back off up to an hour; the first start queues every usable track with no cells. An effort's `visibility` is the narrowest of the activity's and its Map and Pace/Speed categories' tiers (`DetailCategory.currentTiersOfChannels`, from indexed rows only), kept current by `tiers` jobs, and every effort query applies `visibleTo` on the effort and on its activity's row (so a narrowed or taken-down activity hides its efforts before its job runs) and `served` on the segment; `best` is judged among the efforts the same viewer may see. `getActivitySegments` is `NotFound` for an activity the viewer can't see. Efforts are never stored with a wall-clock time: only the offset from the activity's start, the elapsed time, and the local date (`started_on`).
- **Every activity read is filtered by tier.** `activities.visibility` is the tier a row is served at (`public` for the repo, else its space's key), and `visibleTo(viewer)` (`api/TierAccess.kt`) ANDs into every activity query: timeline, author activities (both orders), `getActivity` (a hidden activity is `NotFound`), `getLikes`, `getComments`, recordings and `activitiesCount`. It admits readers as `SpaceAccess` does, from effective follows; an unknown tier is the athlete's only, and a null viewer sees public rows. Likes are checked at serve time for their giver too: `hydrate`'s counts and `getLikes` join likes to the activity (`admittedLikes`) and apply `visibleTo(Likes.authorDid)`, the per-row form, so a like counts only while its giver may see the activity at its current tier, wherever the like is stored (repo or the giver's `interactions` space, `likes.origin`). Comments get the same giver check plus their audience: `hydrate`'s counts and `getComments`' list join comments to the activity (`admittedComments`) and apply `commentsVisibleTo`, which is `visibleTo(Comments.authorDid)` and `visibleTo(viewer)` against `comments.audience` (the visibility the comment was written for, normalised at index time: absent is `public`, unknown is `self`), and for a reply also against its thread's root's audience while the root is indexed on the same activity (`rootAdmits`, through the `Roots` alias `admittedComments` left-joins). Clients cap a reply by its parent, so only the root is checked here: a dishonest client's wider reply still never reaches past it. Tiers nest, so a comment shows at the narrower of the activity's current tier and its audience: it follows a narrowing, but a widening never shows it wider than written. `comments.origin` scopes deletes and `dropSpace` like `likes.origin`. An athlete hides a comment on their own activity with `feed.commentHide` (`comment_hides`, keyed by `(uri, origin)`, so the repo's and the space's copies are separate rows). The indexer keeps a hide only at rkey = `commentHideRkey(subject)` (`core/atproto`), checked before its transaction since the hash suspends; whether its author is the activity's athlete is checked at serve time (`hiddenByAthlete`), so a hide may arrive before its comment and a stranger's is inert. A hidden comment is never counted (`hydrate` ANDs `not(hiddenByAthlete())`) and is listed, `hidden = true`, only for the athlete and its commenter (`commentsListedFor`). Every comment query, count or list, must apply both, or the count stops matching the listed comments that aren't hidden. `getActivity` lists no comments (it still writes `comments: []` for older clients). **Replies are comment rows in a thread.** Threads are one level deep: `comments.thread_uri` is the record's `reply.root` (its own `uri` for a top-level comment), `parent_uri` its `reply.parent`, and `thread_cid`/`parent_cid` complete the strong refs views serve. The indexer skips a reply whose root or parent isn't a `feed.comment` URI with an rkey (dropping an earlier version of it from the same origin, so an edit can't keep it listed), but doesn't check that the root is indexed or on the same activity: threads are grouped within `subject_uri`, so a stray root just leaves its replies under a placeholder. `getComments` lists them oldest first in three modes, all through the same `commentsListedFor` filter, so the count (which includes replies) matches. A page (`threadPage`) groups the listed rows by `thread_uri` with a `GROUP BY … HAVING` keyset on `(MIN(threadPlace), thread_uri)`. A root is placed by its `created_at`, and a reply by the later of its `created_at` and its first `indexed_at` (comment upserts keep that one on replay), so a backdated reply can't move its thread earlier and a new reply never moves a thread across a page already read. A thread whose root isn't listed (a placeholder, `root` absent, revealing nothing) is placed by its earliest listed reply. The page then reads each thread's first three listed replies with a limited query per thread, plus one grouped count for `replyCount`, and sets a `repliesCursor` when there are more. `comments` holds the page's top-level comments for older clients. With `root`, a page of that thread's replies (a keyset on `(created_at, uri)` with `keysetAfter`, whose cursor comes from the last row read; `commentViews` builds a view for every row, showing an author with no `actors` row as `handle.invalid`). With `uris`, just those comments and replies (at most 25, no cursor), which clients use to tell whether the AppView has caught up with their own changes on pages they haven't loaded; `uris` with `root` is `InvalidRequest`. The grouping reads every listed row on the activity (`comments_subject_idx`; `HAVING` can't seek), while the reply fetches use `comments_thread_idx`. A recording counts (hidden from feeds, listed under its primary) only at its primary's visibility.
- **Activities have one URI wherever they're held.** A space-held activity is indexed at `at://{did}/app.activitytracker.feed.activity/{rkey}`, like a repo one, with `origin` = `repo` or the space URI. Mid-move one URI has two origins: the narrower tier's copy is the row, the other waits in `record_shadows`, and a delete removes only its own origin's copy, promoting the shadow (`Indexer.place`/`delete`). Zones work the same way (`activity_zones.record` keeps the raw record for that). Streams and details don't: an activity has one of each per tier (`feed/detail-visibility`), so `activity_streams` and `activity_details` are keyed by `(uri, origin)`, each origin's copy a row of its own, and a delete removes only its own origin's row. `feed.activityDetails` is indexed only from tier spaces; a repo copy is ignored.
- **Activity details** are served in `activityView.details` (`Views.detailsOf`, one batched query per page): only rows whose tier `visibleTo` admits the viewer, for activities the viewer may see, widest first. Clients lay them over `record`.
- **Activity streams are served only through `ActivityStreamsViews`.** `getActivityStreams` serves the union of the activity's streams rows the viewer is admitted to, merged widest first with a narrower tier's channels winning and laps and legs merged by index (`mergeStreams` in `core/lexicon`), lists each in `parts`, and gives `visibility` as the widest. It checks each row's visibility, which is where the record is stored, as for activities (`public` in the repo, the space's tier otherwise, an unknown tier `self`; a `visibility` field in the record is ignored), and hides streams whose indexed activity the viewer can't see. Zones are a record of their own (`feed.activityZones`, `activity_zones`), which the athlete stores at the narrowest of the heart rate, power and training visibilities; `getActivityStreams` includes them only when `visibleTo` admits the viewer to the zones row's own tier. Zone fields in a streams record are ignored. The streams' data blob comes through `StreamSource` (the repo's PDS, or the space as a member) and is cached by CID in `activity_stream_data`.
- **`checkUserAccess` is the managing-app callout.** An athlete's PDS calls it before issuing a credential to one of their `app.activitytracker.space.athlete` spaces. Its tokens are addressed to `managingAppService` (`<serviceDid>#activitytracker_space`), so the route uses the separate managing-app verifier (`verifier.forAudiences`), and AppView tokens never work there or the reverse. `SpaceAccess` decides by tier (`AthleteSpace` skey): `public` admits anyone, `followers` uses `follows` (an effective follow of the athlete, also the rule for Followers streams), `mutuals` uses `isMutual` (effective follows both ways; both in `api/ActorDetails.kt`, also the rule for Mutuals streams), and everything else, every `write` and an issuer other than the space's authority are denied. The AppView's own DID may read all four tiers, `self` included, and the `interactions` space (which admits no one else), so it can sync them; it's denied `write` and unknown keys like anyone. A denial is `200 {"authorized": false}`, never an error.
- **Blocks hide two athletes from each other; mutes hide one from the muter.** `blocks` holds an athlete's `graph.block` from their interactions space (`origin` its URI) and their `app.bsky.graph.block` from their repo (`origin` `repo`), each kept only at rkey = subject for the app's. A pair is blocked while any row names it either way (`blocked()`, `api/Blocks.kt`), and that sits in the shared predicates: `admits` (so `visibleTo`, Public included, and through the giver check every like and comment between them), `effectiveFollow()` (so tier spaces, counts, lists and the timeline) and `SpaceAccess.canRead` (every key). Viewer-versus-giver checks on third parties' activities are `notBlocked`/`notMuted` in `commentsVisibleTo` and the like queries; search, follow lists and `getProfile` filter by the requested DID, and an athlete who blocks the viewer answers `ProfileNotFound`, never "blocked" (`viewer.blocking` is only the viewer's own). Mutes (`mutes`) apply only to the muter's timeline and comments. Jetstream sends every Bluesky block on the network, so `Indexer` keeps one only when its author already has an `actors` row, before `ensureActor`, and `BskyBlockBackfill` lists an actor's existing ones from their PDS while `actors.bsky_blocks_synced_at` is null. Nothing is refused at index time: hiding happens when reading, so an unblock restores everything.
- **Only effective follows count.** An athlete whose profile has `followPolicy: approve` (stored as `actors.follow_policy`; any other value is null, meaning open) counts a follow only once they publish a `graph.followApproval` for the follower. Every query that reads `follows`, except for the viewer's own follow record (`viewer.following`), ANDs in `effectiveFollow()` (`api/ActorDetails.kt`): timeline authors, counts, follower and follow lists, `follows`/`isMutual`. The indexer keeps an approval only at rkey = its subject's DID, so there's one per pair and deleting it removes the follower. `getFollowRequests` lists the pending ones, declined included: declines live in the athlete's `self` space and never reach the AppView.
- **Repos are untrusted input.** The `Indexer` decodes with the shared `AtJson` models, drops records that fail to decode (it catches `IllegalArgumentException` and `SerializationException`), and truncates free-text columns with `.take(n)` so they fit the column. New record handlers do the same.
- **Indexing is idempotent.** Every write is an upsert or delete keyed by AT-URI, so rewinding the Jetstream cursor replays safely. Never use a plain `insert` for record rows. Every new record table also needs a delete branch in `Indexer.delete` and a line in `deleteActor`.
- **Recordings (`feed.activity#recordingOf`) are honored only within one author's repo.** The indexer stores `recording_of` only for a link to another of the author's own activities. `shownInFeeds` (`api/Views.kt`) hides a recording from feeds only while the activity it names is indexed and isn't itself a recording, and `hydrate` lists a primary's `recordings`. Queries that look for duplicates (`getAuthorActivitiesByStart`) return recordings too.
- **Combined activities (`feed.activity#combined`) are derived from the combined record alone; members are never rewritten.** The indexer stores each listed member of the author's own (at most 10, not itself) as a claim in `activity_members`, replaced when the combined record is re-indexed and dropped with it. Whether a claim counts is checked at read time (`api/Members.kt`, `validClaim`), since members may be indexed after their combined activity and move tier on their own: the combined activity is indexed with the member's author and visibility, the member claims nothing itself, and no smaller (earlier-TID) combined URI of the same author and visibility claims it. `shownInFeeds` and `activitiesCount` leave out validly claimed members (`isMember`), `hydrate` serves `members` and `combinedInto`, and a combined activity's like and comment counts, `getLikes` and `getComments` take its members' too (`interactionSubjects`), under the same giver, audience and hide rules. `getAuthorActivitiesByStart` still returns members, with `combinedInto`, for duplicate searches.
- **The AppView syncs athletes' tier spaces as a member** (`sync/SpaceSyncer.kt`), with its own identity: `APPVIEW_SIGNING_KEY` (P-256, hex; `scripts/dev.sh` generates it into `docker/.env`, the Gradle `run` task reads it) is published as `#atproto` in `did.json` and signs the delegation tokens `SpaceCredentials` exchanges (`SignedDelegationTokens`). Without the key nothing is synced and only public activities exist. Clients announce a space with `notifySpace` (service auth from its authority; only `followers`, `mutuals`, `self` and `interactions`); the syncer joins it, registers for `notifyWrite` at `<serviceDid>#activitytracker_space_syncer` (its own audience, so AppView and managing-app tokens never work there), backfills (head revision from `listRepoOps`, then `listRecords`, since the op log may be compacted) and then pulls ops after the last indexed revision. A space that hasn't synced for an hour (backoff, downtime) is backfilled again instead, dropping indexed records it no longer lists, since the ops it missed may be compacted away. The authority's PDS sends each notification once, so every space is also pulled every 15 minutes and its registration renewed 2 h before expiry. A failing space backs off on its own; `SpaceDeleted` drops it and its rows. A PDS caches DID documents, so a new or rotated key is picked up only on a later retry.
- **Activity photos (`feed.activity#media`).** `boundedPhotos` keeps the `#image` members whose blob is WebP or JPEG, within the lexicon's size, with a real CID (other union members stay in the repo but not the index), and `activity_media` lists them. On a public activity, `GET /img/media/{did}/{cid}` (`api/BlobRedirects.kt`) redirects to the PDS's `sync.getBlob`, like the avatar route. A non-public activity's photo URL is signed for the viewer the view was built for (`PrivateMediaTokens`, since image loaders send no service auth); the route checks the signature, re-checks `visibleTo`, fetches the blob from the space as a member (`SpaceBlobs`, which keeps recent blobs in memory by CID) and answers `Cache-Control: private`, never redirecting.
- **Don't trust client timestamps for ordering.** Feed order uses `sort_at = min(createdAt, indexedAt)`, so a client can't pin a record to the top by dating it in the future.
- **Migrations.** These are Flyway files in `src/main/resources/db/migration`, named `V<n>__<description>.sql`. Never edit a migration that is already committed. Add the next `V<n+1>`, because the local Postgres has already applied the old one and Flyway rejects a changed checksum. Keep SQL portable: tests run it on H2 in `MODE=PostgreSQL`, so use no Postgres-only types or functions. Timestamps are `BIGINT` epoch millis. SQL that only PostgreSQL can run (extensions, GIN/trigram indexes) goes in `db/vendor/postgresql`, which `DatabaseFactory.migrate` adds for that database only. Its versions share one sequence with `db/migration` (`MigrationVersionsTest` checks).
- **Routes.** Add queries with `xrpcQuery(X.method, verifier) { params, viewer -> ... }` in `Routes.kt`. Auth is optional at that level. The only procedures are the sync callouts (`notifySpace`, `notifyWrite`, in `spaceSyncRoutes` via `xrpcProcedure`): clients write records to their own repos and spaces, never through the AppView. A handler that needs a viewer throws `AuthException`. Map client errors to `XrpcError(status, "ErrorName", message)`.
- **Tests** (`AppViewTest`) feed `JetstreamEvent.Commit`s into a real `Indexer` over H2 and then read back through `testApplication`. Test new indexing and views the same way, end to end.
